What is Dysphagia?

Dysphagia is difficulty or discomfort when swallowing. It affects many Indian adults due to stroke, aging, neurological conditions, or head and neck issues.

Common Symptoms

  • Choking or coughing while eating/drinking
  • Feeling food stuck in throat or chest
  • Pain when swallowing
  • Wet or gurgly voice after eating
  • Weight loss or dehydration
  • Recurrent chest infections

Why Seek Help?

Untreated dysphagia can lead to aspiration (food entering lungs), pneumonia, malnutrition, and reduced quality of life. Speech-language pathologists (SLPs) are trained to assess and treat swallowing disorders.

Indian Diet Modifications

Managing dysphagia with familiar Indian foods. Always follow your SLP's recommendations—these are general guidelines.

✓ Generally Safe (When Modified)

  • Dal — well-cooked, smooth, add water/cream
  • Curd/Dahi — plain, smooth consistency
  • Rice — soft, mushy khichdi or congee
  • Sabzi — puréed vegetables, avoid chunks
  • Paneer — grated or blended in gravy
  • Suji/Upma — soft, well-moistened
  • Kheer — smooth, avoid nuts

✗ Use Caution or Avoid

  • Dry chapati/roti (choking risk)
  • Hard papad, chips, dry snacks
  • Nuts, whole seeds, coconut chunks
  • Stringy vegetables (okra, spinach stems)
  • Dry rice, biryani with large pieces
  • Thin liquids (may require thickening)

Diet modifications depend on your swallow assessment. A speech-language pathologist will give you personalised advice.

Swallowing Exercises

These exercises may be recommended by your SLP. Do not start without professional guidance.

Effortful Swallow

Swallow forcefully, squeezing throat muscles. Helps strengthen swallow coordination.

Chin Tuck

Tuck chin to chest when swallowing. Can reduce aspiration in some people.

Mendelsohn Maneuver

Hold your Adam's apple up for a few seconds mid-swallow to strengthen laryngeal movement.

Masako Maneuver

Stick tongue out, hold gently with teeth, then swallow. Strengthens posterior tongue.

Exercises must be prescribed by a speech-language pathologist based on your individual assessment. Incorrect use can worsen symptoms.
